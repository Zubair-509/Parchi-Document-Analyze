## 1. Executive Summary

Parchi is an AI-powered prescription companion for patients in Pakistan that does three things no other product in the country does together:

**1. Understands** — Upload a prescription photo. Parchi reads every medicine and explains it in plain Urdu: what it is for, when and how to take it, what to watch out for, and a visual daily schedule.

**2. Informs** — For each medicine, Parchi identifies the active chemical formula and shows same-formula alternatives across three price tiers (Affordable, Medium, Expensive) so patients who cannot afford the branded version know exactly what to ask for.

**3. Empowers** — Parchi shows the clinical evidence behind every prescribed medicine — whether it is on the WHO Essential Medicines List, what it is commonly indicated for, and how strong its evidence base is. For patients who upload a test report alongside their prescription, Parchi explains each test value in plain Urdu and generates specific questions the patient can bring back to their doctor.

Pakistan has one of the lowest medication adherence rates in South Asia. The root causes are confusion, cost, and information asymmetry between doctor and patient. Parchi closes all three gaps — without ever replacing the doctor, diagnosing a condition, or recommending that a patient skip a medicine.

## 2. Problem Statement

### 2.1 The Confusion Problem

In Pakistan, a typical doctor visit lasts under five minutes. The patient leaves with a handwritten prescription — sometimes legible, often not — and the responsibility to understand it falls entirely on them. Patients stop antibiotics early, take medicines at wrong times, skip warnings, and abandon courses they never understood.

### 2.2 The Affordability Problem

Pakistani doctors are trained in a system that incentivizes branded prescribing. Pharmaceutical representatives visit clinics, distribute samples, and build brand loyalty. A prescription almost always specifies a brand, not the active formula. The patient has no way of knowing that a legally equivalent generic exists for a fraction of the cost. A chronic patient managing diabetes and hypertension may pay Rs 4,500/month for branded prescriptions when generic equivalents of identical formula cost Rs 900.

### 2.3 The Over-Prescription Problem (NEW in v2.1)

Pakistan has a documented and widely discussed problem of over-prescription. Patients routinely leave clinics with prescriptions containing:

- **Vitamins and tonics** prescribed without deficiency confirmed by testing — Neurobion injections, multivitamin syrups, iron supplements handed to every patient regardless of need.
- **Digestive enzymes** (Unienzyme, Novalac) with limited clinical evidence for most uses, prescribed as routine "supporting" medicines.
- **Antibiotics for viral illnesses** — azithromycin and amoxicillin prescribed for common colds and flu where they have no clinical effect.
- **Unnecessary combinations** — three medications prescribed where one would be sufficient.

The patient has no way to evaluate this. They have no framework to ask: *"Doctor, why did you prescribe this vitamin? My test report does not show a deficiency."* The information asymmetry is total — the doctor knows everything, the patient knows nothing. This asymmetry is exactly what enables over-prescription to persist.

**The critical design boundary:** Parchi does not tell patients whether their prescription is correct. It does not evaluate the doctor's judgment. What it does is give patients publicly available medical information about every medicine on their prescription — the same information a knowledgeable friend with a medical background would share — so patients can form intelligent questions and have an informed conversation with their doctor.

### 2.4 The Test Report Problem (NEW in v2.1)

Millions of patients in Pakistan receive blood test reports they cannot interpret. CBC values, HbA1c levels, lipid profiles, urine reports — the numbers sit on a paper while the doctor says "everything looks fine" without explanation, or prescribes more medicine the patient does not understand. When patients do not understand their test results, they cannot participate in their own healthcare. A patient who sees their HbA1c is 9.2% without understanding what that means will not take their diabetes management seriously.

### 2.5 Why Existing Solutions Fall Short

| Solution | What It Does | What It Misses |
|----------|-------------|----------------|
| Drug reference apps | Database of medicines | English-only; no evidence summary; no Urdu; no test reports |
| Online pharmacies | Sell medicines | No education; no alternatives; no evidence context |
| Pharmacists | Sometimes explain | Crowded; rushed; patients don't know what to ask |
| Doctors | Prescribe | Under five minutes; no time to explain; incentives misaligned |

## 3. Goals and Non-Goals

### 3.1 Goals (MVP — v2.1)

- Upload prescription photo → AI identifies each medicine
- Bilingual explanation: purpose, dosage, timing, food instructions, warnings, Urdu schedule
- Active formula identification (INN) + three-tier generic price comparison
- Evidence layer for each medicine:
  - WHO Essential Medicines List status
  - Common clinical indications
  - Evidence strength rating (Strong / Common Practice / Limited)
  - Specific "Ask your doctor" question for the patient
- Test report reader: upload blood test / lab report image → AI explains each value in plain Urdu with normal range context and doctor questions
- Prominent bilingual safety disclaimers on all features
- "Unclear" flagging — never guess; always direct to pharmacist or doctor

### 3.2 Non-Goals (MVP)

- Diagnosing any illness or condition
- Recommending that a patient skip, substitute, or stop any medicine
- Rendering a verdict on whether any prescription is correct or over-prescribed
- Identifying drug-drug interactions (deferred — requires medical review board)
- Personalized medicine recommendations based on symptoms
- Push notification reminders
- Persistent history across sessions
- Direct medicine ordering

> The features in v2.1 are about giving patients public medical information and empowering them to ask better questions. They are never about evaluating a specific doctor's clinical decision for a specific patient.

## 6. Functional Requirements

### FR-01 — Prescription Image Upload

- User uploads JPG, PNG, or HEIC of a prescription from camera or gallery
- Image is processed by multimodal AI
- Processing indicator shows during analysis
- Maximum file size: 10MB

### FR-02 — AI Prescription Reading

- AI extracts each distinct medicine from the prescription image
- Returns the full medicine object (see Data Model, Section 11)
- "Unclear" medicines flagged explicitly — AI never guesses

### FR-03 — Medicine Card Display

One card per medicine showing: name, generic name, formula badge, purpose, time chips, food badge, dosage, duration, warning, side effects (expandable), evidence layer (Section FR-05), generic alternatives panel (Section FR-04), Urdu explanation.

### FR-04 — Active Formula + Generic Price Comparison

- AI identifies International Nonproprietary Name (INN) for each medicine
- Three-tier price display: Affordable → Medium → Expensive
- Affordable tier shown first and with highest visual prominence
- Savings callout showing difference vs prescribed brand
- In-panel disclaimer directing to pharmacist before switching
- Hardcoded fallback database for top 30 common Pakistani medicines

### FR-05 — Evidence Layer (NEW in v2.1)

For every medicine where the AI can identify the formula:

**A. WHO Essential Medicines Badge**
- If on WHO Essential Medicines List 23rd Edition (2023): display green badge "✓ WHO Essential Medicine"
- If not on WHO list: no badge shown (absence is information the patient can draw their own conclusions from; Parchi does not label medicines "not essential")
- If status unknown: no badge

**B. Common Indications Summary**
- 2–3 bullet points: the conditions this medicine is most commonly and clinically prescribed for
- Written in plain, factual English and Urdu
- Example for Azithromycin: "Bacterial respiratory infections (pneumonia, bronchitis) | Bacterial skin infections | NOT effective for viral infections like common cold or flu"
- The "NOT effective for" line is factual, not evaluative — it is a published medical fact

**C. Evidence Strength Badge**
A simple badge with three values — this is a fact about the medicine globally, not an evaluation of the specific prescription:

| Badge | Meaning | Color |
|-------|---------|-------|
| Strong Evidence | Supported by multiple large clinical trials | Green |
| Common Practice | Widely used; moderate or mixed trial evidence | Amber |
| Limited Evidence | Few quality trials; routine use is debated | Grey |

- Source basis: WHO, Cochrane reviews, NICE guidelines, international pharmacopeia
- This is publicly available medical information — not a clinical opinion on the patient's prescription

**D. "Ask Your Doctor" Prompt**
- One specific, polite, actionable question the patient can bring to their next consultation
- The question is about information-gathering, never confrontational
- Examples:
  - "What specific condition is this [medicine] treating in my case?"
  - "I understand antibiotics treat bacterial infections — can you tell me if my infection is bacterial?"
  - "Is this vitamin supplement based on a deficiency shown in my test results?"
  - "Are all six of these medicines necessary, or could any be monitored rather than treated right now?"
- Displayed in both English and Urdu

**E. Display Rules for Evidence Layer**
- Evidence layer is expandable/collapsible — default: collapsed on mobile, expanded on desktop
- Evidence layer is NEVER framed as "you should question this prescription"
- Every piece of information is framed as public medical knowledge the patient has a right to
- The word "unnecessary" never appears. The word "useless" never appears.
- Parchi presents information. The patient draws their own conclusions and brings questions to their doctor.

### FR-06 — Test Report Reader (NEW in v2.1)

A separate upload flow for lab/blood test reports, accessible via a dedicated tab or button.

- User uploads an image of a blood test, CBC, urine report, or other lab result
- AI reads each test value from the report
- For each test value, displays a card (see Data Model Section 11.3)
- Cards are color-coded by status: Normal (green), High (coral), Low (blue), Borderline (amber)
- Each card includes plain Urdu explanation + "Ask your doctor" question for abnormal values
- Urgency indicator: Routine | Discuss Soon | Discuss Urgently
- Overall summary: how many values are normal, borderline, or flagged
- Prominent disclaimer: Parchi explains values — it does not diagnose conditions

**Test report framing rules:**
- NOT: "Your high sugar suggests diabetes"
- YES: "HbA1c measures average blood sugar over 3 months. Your value of 8.2% is above the standard normal range of below 5.7%. This is worth discussing with your doctor."
- The patient receives context, not a diagnosis

### FR-07 — Daily Schedule View

Visual four-block schedule (Morning / Afternoon / Evening / Night) with food context.

### FR-08 — Safety Disclaimer

- Full-page bilingual disclaimer on every results screen
- In-panel disclaimer in generic alternatives section
- In-panel disclaimer in evidence layer section
- Disclaimer in test report results
- None are collapsible

### FR-09 — Unclear Handling

- "Unclear" medicines show warning state with no alternatives, no formula, no evidence data
- "Unclear" test values show: "This value could not be read clearly. Ask your pharmacist or doctor to confirm."

## 7. Non-Functional Requirements

### 7.1 Performance

- Prescription analysis (medicine + formula + alternatives + evidence): within 15 seconds
- Test report analysis: within 12 seconds
- App load: within 3 seconds

### 7.2 Data Accuracy

- All prices labeled "Approximate — verify at pharmacy"
- Evidence strength labels attributed to general medical knowledge, not patient-specific advice
- Test report values shown as read — patient directed to doctor for interpretation

### 7.3 Privacy

- Prescription and test report images processed and immediately discarded — never stored
- No personal health data retained at MVP stage
- Explicit notice that images are not stored

### 7.4 Accessibility

- WCAG 2.1 Level AA throughout
- 48×48px minimum tap targets
- Color never sole differentiator (always icon + label + color)
- Urdu text: `lang="ur"`, `dir="rtl"`, `line-height: 2.2`

## 8. System Architecture

### 8.1 Prescription Flow

```
User uploads prescription image
    ↓
Frontend (Bolt.new / Lovable)
    ↓
Backend (Gemini API key server-side)
    ↓
Gemini Vision API — enriched prompt v2.1
(medicine + formula + alternatives + evidence layer)
    ↓
Backend parses JSON
    ↓
Frontend checks formula against fallback database
(overrides price data for top 30 medicines)
    ↓
Renders medicine cards with all four layers:
1. Explanation (Urdu + English)
2. Generic alternatives (3 price tiers)
3. Evidence layer (WHO badge, indications, evidence strength, doctor question)
4. Daily schedule
```

### 8.2 Test Report Flow (NEW)

```
User uploads test report image (separate tab/button)
    ↓
Frontend
    ↓
Backend (same Gemini key, different system prompt)
    ↓
Gemini Vision API — test report system prompt
    ↓
Backend parses test value array
    ↓
Frontend renders:
- Summary row (X normal, Y borderline, Z flagged)
- Individual test value cards (color-coded by status)
- Doctor questions for abnormal values
- Disclaimer
```

### 8.3 Technology Stack

| Component | Technology |
|-----------|------------|
| Frontend builder | Bolt.new (primary), Lovable (backup) |
| AI Vision | Google Gemini 1.5 Flash |
| Evidence data source | Gemini training knowledge (WHO, Cochrane, NICE) |
| Fallback price + evidence DB | Hardcoded JSON (top 30 medicines) |
| State management | React useState |
| Storage | In-memory session (MVP) |

## 10. API & Integration Requirements

### 10.1 Google Gemini Vision API

Same endpoint for both flows. Different system prompt per flow.

### 10.2 Updated Prescription System Prompt (v2.1)

```
You are Parchi, a prescription assistant for patients in Pakistan.
When given an image of a prescription, identify each medicine and return:
1. A plain-language explanation in English and Urdu
2. The active chemical formula (INN)
3. Affordable generic alternatives in Pakistan
4. Clinical evidence context (NOT a verdict on the prescription — publicly
   available medical information only)

For each medicine, return this JSON structure:

{
  "medicine_name": "name exactly as written on prescription",
  "standard_name": "common brand or generic name if recognized, else null",
  "active_formula": "INN name (e.g. Paracetamol, Metformin, Ibuprofen). Use null if unknown.",
  "formula_urdu": "Urdu name if commonly known in Pakistan, else null",
  "purpose": "what this medicine is commonly prescribed for (1 sentence, plain language)",
  "dosage": "dosage as written",
  "timing": ["morning" | "afternoon" | "evening" | "night"],
  "food_relation": "before_food" | "after_food" | "with_food" | "anytime",
  "duration": "days if mentioned, else null",
  "common_side_effects": ["1 to 3 key side effects"],
  "important_warning": "critical warning if any, else null",
  "explanation_urdu": "2-sentence plain Urdu explanation for a patient with low health literacy",
  "generic_alternatives": [
    {
      "brand_name": "product name available in Pakistan",
      "manufacturer": "company name if known",
      "price_per_tablet_pkr": number or null,
      "tier": "affordable" | "medium" | "expensive",
      "note": "brief note or null"
    }
  ],
  "evidence": {
    "who_essential": true | false | null,
    "common_indications": [
      "Condition or use 1",
      "Condition or use 2",
      "NOT effective for: [condition] if applicable"
    ],
    "evidence_strength": "strong" | "common_practice" | "limited" | null,
    "evidence_note": "1-sentence factual summary of the evidence base (e.g. 'Supported by multiple WHO-recognized clinical trials' or 'Limited high-quality trials; routine use is debated in international guidelines')",
    "evidence_note_urdu": "same note in plain Urdu",
    "doctor_question_english": "One specific, polite question the patient can ask their doctor about this medicine at their next visit",
    "doctor_question_urdu": "Same question in plain Urdu"
  },
  "confidence": "high" | "medium" | "low"
}

CRITICAL RULES:
1. The evidence section is INFORMATIONAL ONLY. Never say a medicine is
   unnecessary, useless, or should be skipped. Provide publicly available
   medical facts.
2. For who_essential: base on WHO Essential Medicines List 23rd Edition (2023).
3. For evidence_strength: base on existence of robust clinical trials
   (Cochrane, WHO, NICE). Strong = multiple large RCTs. Common_practice =
   widespread use with moderate evidence. Limited = few quality trials,
   debated in guidelines.
4. The doctor_question must be information-seeking, never accusatory.
5. If medicine name is unclear, set confidence to "low", all fields to null.
   Never guess.
6. Output ONLY valid JSON.

Return: { "medicines": [...], "disclaimer": "..." }
Disclaimer text: "Information is for guidance only. Evidence ratings reflect general medical literature, not your specific situation. Always discuss any questions with your doctor or pharmacist. Parchi does not diagnose or prescribe. | یہ معلومات صرف رہنمائی کے لیے ہیں۔ ہمیشہ اپنے ڈاکٹر سے مشورہ کریں۔"
```

```
You are Parchi's test report reader for patients in Pakistan.
When given an image of a laboratory or blood test report, identify each
test value and explain it in plain, non-medical language.

For each test value, return:
{
  "test_name": "name of the test as shown on report",
  "test_name_urdu": "Urdu name if commonly known, else null",
  "patient_value": "the patient's result exactly as shown",
  "unit": "unit of measurement (e.g. mg/dL, g/L, %)",
  "normal_range": "standard normal range as shown on report, or internationally accepted standard if not shown",
  "status": "normal" | "high" | "low" | "borderline" | "unclear",
  "urgency": "routine" | "discuss_soon" | "discuss_urgently",
  "explanation_english": "2 sentences: what this test measures + what the patient's result means in plain language. Do NOT suggest a diagnosis.",
  "explanation_urdu": "Same in plain Urdu",
  "doctor_question": "One specific question to ask the doctor IF the result is not normal. Null if normal.",
  "doctor_question_urdu": "Same in Urdu or null"

CRITICAL RULES:
1. NEVER suggest a diagnosis. NEVER say "this means you have [condition]".
   Say: "this value is above/below the normal range — worth discussing."
2. Urgency rules: discuss_urgently only for values far outside range with
   known clinical urgency (e.g. critically low hemoglobin, very high
   potassium). discuss_soon for moderately abnormal values. routine for
   all normal values.
3. If a value cannot be read clearly, set status to "unclear".
4. Output ONLY valid JSON.

Return: {
  "test_values": [...],
  "summary": {
    "total": number,
    "normal": number,
    "borderline": number,
    "flagged": number,
    "urgent": number
  },
  "disclaimer": "These explanations are for information only. Only your doctor can interpret your results in the context of your full health history. Parchi does not diagnose. | یہ وضاحتیں صرف معلومات کے لیے ہیں۔ نتائج کی تشریح صرف آپ کا ڈاکٹر کر سکتا ہے۔"
}
```

### 10.4 Error Handling Matrix

| Failure Mode | User-Facing Response |
|-------------|----------------------|
| Gemini API failure | "Could not reach AI. Try again or enter manually." |
| No medicines found in image | "No medicines found. Make sure the photo shows the prescription clearly." |
| Image not a prescription | "This doesn't look like a prescription. Please upload a prescription image." |
| Formula identified, no evidence data | Show formula badge; hide evidence section; show "Evidence data unavailable for this medicine." |
| Test report unreadable | "Could not read this report. Try better lighting or a clearer photo." |
| Network timeout | Auto-retry once, then surface error |

## 12. Safety, Compliance & Liability Framework

### 12.1 The Absolute Principle

> **Parchi informs. It never evaluates, recommends, or judges a specific clinical decision made for a specific patient.**

Every feature in Parchi — the medicine explanation, the generic alternatives, the evidence layer, the test report reader — is information display. The same information a pharmacist, a knowledgeable friend, or a medical reference website would provide.

### 12.2 The Evidence Layer — Specific Safety Rules

**What the evidence layer IS:**
- Publicly available medical information about a medicine's recognized uses and evidence base
- A tool to help patients form intelligent questions
- The same kind of information a patient could find on the WHO website or in a drug reference

**What the evidence layer IS NOT:**
- A verdict on whether the doctor's prescription was correct
- A recommendation to skip, discontinue, or question a specific medicine for a specific patient
- A second opinion on a clinical decision

**Forbidden language in evidence layer (never appear in UI or AI output):**
- "This medicine is unnecessary"
- "You can skip this"
- "Your doctor over-prescribed"
- "This is a useless medicine"
- "You do not need this"

**Required framing for Limited Evidence medicines:**
- YES: "Limited clinical trial evidence supports this specific use in international guidelines."
- YES: "Widely prescribed in Pakistan; evidence from large clinical trials is limited."
- NO: "This medicine doesn't work" / "This is not recommended"

### 12.3 The Test Report Reader — Specific Safety Rules

**What the test report reader IS:**
- A translation and context layer — what does this test measure, what does this number mean
- A reference for normal ranges
- A generator of intelligent doctor questions

**What it IS NOT:**
- A diagnostic tool
- A replacement for the doctor's interpretation in context of the full patient history
- An urgency classification that replaces emergency medical advice

**Critical test report rule:** If any value is classified "discuss_urgently," Parchi must show a prominent message: *"This result is significantly outside the normal range. Please contact your doctor today or visit a clinic promptly."* This is one place where Parchi does proactively prompt action — patient safety requires it.

### 12.4 Prepared Q&A for Judges

| Question | Position |
|---------|---------|
| "What if a patient uses the evidence layer to skip a medicine they actually need?" | Parchi never recommends skipping anything. We show publicly available medical information. A patient who decides to skip a medicine makes that choice — the same choice they could make after 10 minutes on WHO's website. We direct them to ask their doctor first. |
| "Aren't you undermining doctors?" | The opposite. A patient who can form an intelligent question has a better doctor consultation. We make the doctor-patient relationship more productive. |
| "What if test report values are misread by AI?" | All values are shown exactly as read from the report. Patient can cross-check against the paper. The disclaimer explicitly states only a doctor can interpret in full context. |
| "Is showing 'Limited Evidence' for a medicine legally actionable by pharma companies?" | We report publicly available information from WHO, Cochrane, and NICE. Reporting factual public health information is legally protected. We are no different from a drug reference publication. |
| "Are you DRAP-regulated?" | We don't sell, dispense, or prescribe. We are an information and education platform — same legal position as a patient education website. |

### 13.1 Revenue Streams

**B2B — Pharmacy and Telehealth (Primary)**

Pharmacies want adherence (complete course = repeat sales). Telehealth platforms want better post-consultation outcomes (patients who understand their prescription return). The evidence layer makes patients smarter about their health, which benefits both.

## 16. Risks & Mitigations

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|------|------------|--------|------------|
| Patient interprets evidence layer as "skip this medicine" | Medium | High | Forbidden language rules strictly enforced. Every evidence section ends with the doctor question prompt — redirecting to the doctor, not away from the medicine. |
| AI misidentifies WHO Essential status | Medium | Medium | Hardcoded evidence data for top 30 medicines overrides AI output. Unknown status shows no badge rather than wrong badge. |
| Test report AI misreads a critical value | Medium | High | Values displayed exactly as read — patient cross-checks against paper. "Discuss urgently" values trigger prominent doctor prompt. Disclaimer is prominent on all results. |
| Branded pharma companies complain about Limited Evidence labels | Medium | Medium | Evidence ratings are attributed to WHO, Cochrane, or international guidelines. Factual public information. Legal team to confirm framing before launch. |
| Doctors feel undermined by evidence feature | Medium | Medium | Position as a patient-education tool that improves consultation quality. Pilot with patient advocacy groups first. Doctor portal (Phase 4) creates an improvement feedback loop. |
| Demo prescription fails evidence layer on common medicines | Low | High | Hardcoded fallback database ensures Augmentin, Panadol, Metformin, vitamins all have pre-curated evidence data that displays reliably. |
| Regulatory attention from DRAP | Low | Medium | Parchi is an information platform. Not selling, dispensing, or prescribing. Identical legal position to a patient-education website. |
